# mg-deathrun-maps

The courses mg-deathrun plays, as JSON documents. Read `../../CLAUDE.md` and `../mg-deathrun/CLAUDE.md` first: the format is defined and validated by mg-deathrun's course document code, and `courses/README.md` is the mapper's copy of it.

**`courses/` is what the game reads**, through a dot-bootstrap link (`mg-deathrun/.gitignore` names it: `# bootstrap-link: mg-deathrun-maps/courses`). The game reads every `.json` there plus the practice course built into it; a refused document is listed by `dr_courses` with the reason, and every other course still plays.

**`tools/build_courses.py` writes every document, and the JSON is committed.** Edit the builder, run it, commit both. `--check` (CI) fails when a committed file differs from what the builder writes. The builder exists because the corridor and the walkway beside it must agree along their whole length: a section lays its floor, trap, button and route points at a cursor, and it refuses two buttons closer than 2.6 m (the nearer would be pressed for the further).

**Split out of mg-deathrun on 2026-10-06**, the way mg-wipeout-maps was split from mg-wipeout; the history of the two courses and the builder before that is in mg-deathrun.

## What a level is here, for the nightly quota

A level is **a course that plays differently from every other**, or a section kind that changes how one plays. In order of preference:

1. **An existing course rebuilt so it plays the way its blurb says.**
2. **A new section kind in the builder**, made of existing part motions (move/turn/spin, solid or lethal), used in at least one course.
3. **A new course** — only with a blurb that says what makes it different, every trap sprung on its victim in `mg-deathrun/examples/headless_courses.tscn`, and a render (`mg-deathrun/tools/shot.sh`).

## Converted community maps

`tools/bsp_to_course.py` (derived from mg-prop-hunt-maps' importer; its reader, `tools/bsp_read.py`, is a shim over dot-map's shared `dotbsp.reader`, so a reading fix goes in dot-map) turns a Source 1 .bsp plus `tools/imports/<id>.json` into `courses/<id>.json`; `build_courses.py --check` ignores these. Rules: a map's `attribution` credits the author, names the page and licence as listed, and says it is removed on request; no author texture, model or sound is carried. Download into a fresh empty scratch dir, never into the repo. A map whose floors are `prop_static` models (Source `death_run`) has nothing the importer sees: pick brush-floored maps. Brush entities (func_breakable floors) that are only floor go in the spec's `static`; shafts whose fan/wind trap is not portable get a `fill` patch. Every skipped trap says why in the spec.

`trigger_push` (a fan's wind) is read since 2026-10-19: a push zone part (`push` m/s along `pushdir`, `look` wind, `hold` from the button's Disable). It only makes a trap when the wind can kill, because every trap is sprung on its victim in `headless_courses`: a sideways wind beside a drop does (dr_sections `t30`); a wind down or up over floor does not (`t32`, `t33`: their shafts are `fill` patches, so they stay skipped).

**Converted 2026-10-20** (all CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 as listed; credit, link and removal offer are in each document's `attribution`; Christian's standing decision is that the label does not stop a conversion, only an author's explicit refusal does): `dr_oreo` (Percolate Oreo, Loller Dan, GameBanana 127264; the full course, 7 traps), `dr_parking_garage` (Bloodhunter4rc, 127127; 5 traps, a doorway cut in the wall the map's wind carries people over), `dr_iceworld` (metz10, 127005; 2 traps that kill once rebuilt, the rest skipped), `dr_fogtown` (Krypt, 127180; the first stretch only, 1 trap), `dr_southpark` (Linoxi, 127198; the first corridor, 1 trap), `dr_horror` (mgproductions, 127000; the first stretch, 7 traps). Iceworld, Fog Town and Southpark are thin on traps (most fail 'every trap catches somebody' once rebuilt from brushes; they are the first thing to improve with hand-written victims and `timing`). Several are only the first stretch of the author's map: later parts are reached by teleport or by a drop longer than the controller's, so the finish is the end of the part that walks. Every skipped trap says why in its spec (`skip`), and `keep` leaves a skipped trap's brushes in place when they are floor.

**The tools** (new 2026-10-20): `tools/draft_spec.py <bsp> <id> "<Name>" "<author>" <gamebanana id> "<licence>" --finish=x,y,z --kill=z` writes a first spec, `tools/plan_route.py <bsp> <spec>` plans `route`, `walkway` and `unwalkable` on a grid of the converted geometry (it lists buttons the activator cannot walk to; `PLAN_PLOT=<png>` draws what is reachable), `tools/bsp_to_course.py` writes the course. The recipe that worked: draft, plan, read the plot, then fix what blocks the walk with the spec keys documented at the top of `bsp_to_course.py` (`static` for breakable floors, `remove`/`remove_sheets` for doorway panes and room walls, `fill` for a gap the map crosses by a script or a ladder, `skip`+`keep` for traps that do not kill once rebuilt), until `headless_courses` is green. A trap's brushes vanish when it is skipped, so a skipped trap whose brushes were floor needs `keep`; one whose brushes were a closed gate must not have it.

**Not converted yet** (follow-up item `maps-conv-deathrun-3`): `dr_aztecan` (n1be, 126828: spec drafted but not committed; the finish hall is cut off from the start hall past the door ring, and removing the spawn door or the x~488 wall and filling the trench did not join them), Simpsons (MrLate, 127183: 15 trigger_teleports and 43 spawns, a teleport hub), the Medieval, Lego, Mario, Family Guy, Spongebob, Gyro, Tribute, Ramesses, Holy Shit and Helix maps (each reaches its later parts by teleport, a mini-game hub, or a 3000 m activator height; Helix's brush model crashes the reader), and the Office (a hub). Fog Town is now a full course (stairs, fills over pits and `static_classes` for func_physbox; 10 traps); South Park beyond the first corridor stays open because the remaining buttons sit on activator-only floors the walkway planner cannot reach without exceeding memory. New spec keys: `stairs`, `static_classes`, `max_drop_m`, `walkway_drops` (documented at the top of `bsp_to_course.py` and `plan_route.py`). `tools/apply_theme.py` applies a THEMES entry's look to a converted course.

**Looked at and not converted (2026-10-19)**, each with its listing, so nobody redoes the survey:

Fourteen buttons are spread through the runners' own rooms, the activators walk the same floors, and teleports hand people between cages and an exit; there is no corridor, no walkway and no single route to give `route` and `activators`, so it would be a redesign, not an import.
- `DR_Hourglass` v5 by WerewolfUK87 (https://gamebanana.com/mods/74542, CC BY-NC-ND as written on the page, plus: do not edit the map, message the author first): a circular arena round a central platform with a train and phys_thruster traps, again no linear route, and the author asks that nobody edits it, which a rebuilt-from-brushes conversion is. Left for the author's say-so.

## Toys

`Course.toy()` puts one of dot-props' loose toys on the floor: a football in every start room (`_start`) and a pair in each end arena (balls on the ice, barrels on the foundry's banks, a ball each side of the altar), written as the document's `toys`. Loose only: a trampoline would be a way past a trap and anything solid cover in the showdown. mg-deathrun's `headless_courses` checks each stands clear and stays put.

## Publishing

**This repository is a pack of its own, mounted on the server alone.** mg-deathrun's `game.yml` names it under `server_dependencies`; a server mounts it at `res://dot_cloud/<owner>/mg-deathrun-maps/<version>/` and the game adds that mount's `courses/` to its catalogue (`DrModule._add_delivered_courses`). It is never in a client's content sync: a client is sent the one document being played. `.github/workflows/release.yml` runs `build_courses.py --check` and then dot-ci's release with `pack: true` (and `server-compat: false`, because there is no code in it to check).

`project.godot` exists only so dot-ci's release can import this repository and attach the `-pack.zip`; it carries the editor's own header and no comments, because the editor rewrites the file on its first save and a checkout would show it modified.

**Not yet on GitHub** (2026-10-06): the remote is set, the repository is the owner's to create, and nothing is pushed or published.

**`hops` (2026-10-06)** is the first section with no trap: small platforms over a pit, side to side, a running jump apart (2.4 m gaps against a ~4.8 m running jump at mg-deathrun's defaults), so one landing at a time is enough and chaining them (auto_bunny_hop, or timing it) is what it rewards. In both courses, after the falling blocks; mg-deathrun's `headless_courses` stand-in clears both with auto-hop off.

**`swing` and The Temple (2026-10-08)**: the first section whose killer TURNS (a blade on an axle over the corridor, swung 180 degrees down through it and back) and the first that is DODGED rather than jumped: it kills the middle of the corridor and passes over a runner pressed to either wall. A trap may now name a `spared` spot as well as its `victim` (the courses README has it), and mg-deathrun's `headless_courses` stands somebody there and fails the trap if they die. That check found the first blade's design wrong the first time it ran: 5.6 m on an axle 6.4 m up came down through head height at the walls too, because a blade's tip draws a circle, so how low it reaches in the middle and how high it passes at the walls are one number; the builder's docstring has the arithmetic, and the blade is 2.75 m on an axle 3.6 m up. **The Temple** (`dr_temple`) is the course built round it: three blades, a pit, floor spikes, stepping stones over green water, a ceiling, a hop stretch and one closing wall, ending in a sunken court round a three-step altar (`temple_arena`). Every trap is sprung on its victim, every blade misses its spared runner, and a stand-in runs the course to the end; rendered with `tools/shot.sh --view=course --dr-course-ids=dr_temple` and `--view=trap --trap=tp_first_blade --after=0.5`.
